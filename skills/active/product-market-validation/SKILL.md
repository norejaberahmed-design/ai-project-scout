# Product and market validation

## Purpose
Help the user decide whether to build, test, change, or abandon an idea based on evidence of demand and economics rather than enthusiasm.

## Procedure
1. Define the exact customer segment, painful job/problem, current workaround, buyer, and who controls the budget.
2. Find evidence of the problem in customer discussions, job posts, procurement records, competitor reviews, search demand, and existing spending. Prefer direct evidence over broad market-size reports.
3. Identify competitors and substitutes, including spreadsheets, messaging apps, manual services, and doing nothing.
4. Determine whether buyers can be reached cheaply and whether they have a reason to switch now.
5. Estimate realistic pricing, acquisition cost, delivery/support effort, hosting/API cost, payment fees, and gross margin. Mark estimates and assumptions clearly.
6. Design the cheapest test that can produce behavioral evidence: a targeted outreach batch, landing page with a clear offer, paid pilot request, deposit where appropriate, or manual concierge service.
7. Define success and stop criteria before the test. Examples include qualified replies, meetings with actual buyers, willingness to pay, or a pilot commitment. Likes and compliments alone are weak evidence.
8. Recommend BUILD, TEST FIRST, PIVOT, or LEAVE THE IDEA. State the evidence and the next concrete action.
9. Do not build a full app before demand is supported, unless the user explicitly requests a prototype for a separate reason.

## Required honesty
- Do not invent customer interviews, market numbers, sales, or competitor pricing.
- Distinguish observed evidence from hypotheses and estimates.
- Include a realistic downside scenario and ongoing costs.
- Respect the user's budget, time, location, language, and legal constraints when known.

## Tests
Evaluate one idea with some real evidence and one with almost no evidence. The first should have a measurable validation plan; the second should receive a clear low-cost test or a "leave it" recommendation rather than a confident market claim.
